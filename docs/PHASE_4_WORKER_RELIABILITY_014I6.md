# Phase 4 #014I-6: dormant worker reliability

## Implemented changes

Draft migration `services/api/migrations/0012_dormant_worker_reliability.sql` adds a lease generation and a revised worker transition RPC. It revokes the dedicated worker role's access to the earlier caller-token transition and grants it access to the new function; it does not create a login, enable direct capsules or change the 0006 `CHECK (false)` gate. The function returns distinct outcomes for successful `leased`, `prepared`, `sent`, `retry`, `failed`, `cancelled` and `expired` operations; a denied, locked, stale or otherwise inapplicable operation returns no row. Terminal states clear ciphertext and lease fields. Database function output is **provisional until its transaction commits**.

`transition_committed` in `services/api/app/invitation_delivery_worker.py` is an unwired DB-API adapter. It requires a caller-supplied fresh, dedicated, non-autocommit connection; validates the returned row and requested job/token/generation; commits before returning an acknowledgement; and closes the cursor and connection. A commit exception raises the generic `TransitionOutcomeUnknown`, never a success, and the adapter does not retry. Empty results are returned as `None` only after a successful commit; they do not identify why the operation was inapplicable. The factory's promise to supply a fresh, isolated connection is a contract, not something generic DB-API can prove. No production connection factory is configured, and `run_delivery()` still fails closed.

## State transitions and lease guarantees

The database generates a fresh random version-4 UUID when acquiring a lease; the caller cannot choose a token for that action. It also increments a per-job `lease_generation` atomically under the outbox row lock. Every `prepare`, `sent`, `retry` and `fail` requires both the exact token and generation and an unexpired 60-second lease. A worker that crashes consumes an attempt; once its lease expires a new worker may lease the job with a fresh token and higher generation. Expired or stale workers cannot apply transitions. A rolled-back lease and its acknowledgement do not persist. Random UUIDs have 122 random bits; collisions are improbable, not impossible. A database restore can roll back the generation, so independent fresh random state, operational restore fencing and key/entropy controls remain necessary.

The existing capsule → invitation → outbox lock order and `SKIP LOCKED` behavior remain. Eligibility and `clock_timestamp()` are checked after locks, including cancellation, deletion, revocation, claim, digest presence and invitation expiry. An invalid invitation can return a terminal cancellation/expiry acknowledgement instead of a payload. Attempts remain capped at five; retries use database-time backoff of 30, 60, 120 and 240 seconds before the cap. The SQL test also checks retry exhaustion, `sent` versus subsequent no-op, expired tokens and recovery. Privileged SQL callers and incorrect role membership can bypass the intended worker boundary; production privileges have not been verified.

An acknowledgement means only that the supplied connection reported a successful commit. A lost connection or timeout during commit can mean either commit or rollback; the adapter reports an unknown outcome and does not retry. Durable storage settings, a real driver/transport and reconciliation of that uncertainty have not been tested. Callers must not treat a raw function row, an empty result, or an exception as a committed success. Lease tokens, addresses and encrypted payloads are sensitive and must be excluded from logs and traces.

## Cancellation and dispatch policy

Immediately before any future dispatch, the worker must make a fresh `prepare` call, receive and validate its committed acknowledgement, authenticate/decrypt the payload and verify its digest. If cancellation, claim, revocation, deletion or expiry is visible to that database check, or a competing transaction holds a needed row lock, no new send is authorized by that check. The local fake-provider tests only call the fake after the commit-aware adapter closes its connection; no database lock is held across the simulated send.

The check and an external provider call cannot be atomic without holding a database lock across the network, which this design does not do. A cancellation, claim, revocation, expiry or lease expiry can occur after the committed check but before the provider accepts the email. If a provider accepts an email, it cannot be recalled. The copied claim RPC was tested to reject a late invitation after cancellation, revocation or expiry, and to reject a duplicate claim after a prior claim. These local fixture results depend on the real claim path retaining its server-side checks; no public claim or worker is enabled. A stale worker can still physically send after its lease expires even though database fencing rejects its later update.

A provider timeout may mean an email was accepted. The fake test demonstrates that a manual retry can produce duplicate emails; no automatic retry or exactly-once delivery is claimed. A future provider integration needs an explicit idempotency/reconciliation policy, safe content and independent review. The claim identity requirement remains unresolved: the existing bearer-secret claim draft does not prove the claimant's current email matches the intended address.

## Remaining limitations and next task

There is no production worker loop, database connection factory, managed key provider, real email transport, provider idempotency or reconciliation process. The SQL fixture uses a simplified Auth stub and redirected functions; it does not prove managed Auth/session races, production grants, PostgREST or a genuine email provider. Cancellation after the last check can still race with a send, and no external fencing or recall is possible. Terminal ciphertext cleanup is not physical erasure, and external logging, tracing, WAL and backups remain unreviewed. Database-time checks, authorization and the 0006 gate must remain in force.

**Next task:** independently review the cancellation and identity policy, then build a separately scoped local-only DB-driver adapter test with actual commit/disconnect ambiguity and a fake provider's idempotency/reconciliation scenarios. Verify production-style role provisioning and restore fencing before considering any worker wiring. Keep `run_delivery`, direct routes, the database gate and real email disabled until separate authorization and security review.
