# Phase 4 #014H-3: recipient identity decision

## Recommendation

Choose **B: an explicit mailbox invitation**, subject to the product owner accepting its identity semantics before implementation. Generate a high-entropy, single-use invitation secret for each direct capsule, deliver it to the sender-entered mailbox, and atomically bind the authenticated recipient's account when the secret is consumed. The secret proves possession of a delivered bearer capability; it does **not** prove that the account's current confirmed email matches the intended address, that the person receiving it is the sender's intended person, or that the mailbox has not been compromised, forwarded or reassigned.

This is a recommendation, not an activation decision or a finding that claiming is safe today. The existing design required a current confirmed-email match. Substituting possession of a mailbox-delivered secret is a material change to that identity rule and requires explicit product/security approval. If a current confirmed-email match remains mandatory, B alone does not satisfy it, and the Auth transaction-boundary blocker remains.

Why B: it avoids making a new claim depend on an unproven managed-Auth email-change lock spanning the claim transaction. It supports a direct invitation journey without a global email-based pending inbox. Its security boundary is explicit and locally testable: a valid invitation secret and verified account must be bound together atomically. For 10–12 users, a small transactional delivery workflow is manageable, but it is still meaningful infrastructure, requires a secure mail channel and adds bearer-secret risks. A is simpler operationally if its identity authority can be proven; that proof is currently missing.

## Evidence and current state

The repository contains separate dormant direct-capsule storage and draft create, cancel and reveal RPCs. Migration 0006 retains a validated `CHECK (false)` on invitations; 0007 retains the gate and switches the invitation foreign key to direct storage; 0008 checks the gate and absence of claim/discovery functions and adds a candidate pending-email index. The backend's disabled flag blocks direct routes, and claim/discovery return unavailable even if the flag is enabled. The mobile Send screen is local-only and Received remains unavailable. No functioning claim or delivery flow exists.

The Auth research records one user-reported completed supported email change and a reported row-lock conflict, neither independently repeated here. They do not establish that every managed Auth email-change, confirmation, deletion and reassignment path serializes with a prospective claim. A local Auth stub cannot establish this. The existing research and `docs/PHASE_4_CLAIM_IMPLEMENTATION_014H2.md` record the evidence and limits; earlier SQL simulations with synthetic bindings and a temporarily removed gate are not successful claim tests.

## Comparison

| Concern | A. Current confirmed-email authorization | B. Explicit mailbox invitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authorization | Binds only after authoritative current confirmed address matches in the claim transaction. Requires proven serialization of all relevant Auth state transitions; a JWT email or separate preflight is insufficient. | Binds the signed-in account that presents the valid secret. Delivery targets the entered address, but the secret is transferable and is not proof of the claimant's current account email or human identity. |
| Email changes | Must recheck and serialize current confirmation and pending-change state through commit; after claim, account binding policy must be explicit. | No current-email comparison is needed for this proposed capability policy. After claim, keep access bound to the original account across an email change; a change does not transfer it to another account. |
| Deletion | Serialize deletion and claim and never treat a historical unbound claim as pending again. | Require an existing active account/profile at claim, serialize with deletion, and preserve terminal `claimed_at` after deletion; no automatic rebinding. Account/session revocation behavior still needs verification. |
| Mailbox reassignment | A newly confirmed owner of a reassigned address can match a pending invitation; even perfect atomicity does not prove they are the intended person. | A new mailbox holder or anyone with a forwarded, leaked or stolen secret can claim before expiry. Short expiry and cancellation reduce exposure but do not remove it. |
| Transactions and races | Atomic Auth identity read plus invitation/capsule locks; actual provider transaction paths and lock ordering must be demonstrated. | Atomic secret check, state checks, one-time consumption and account binding; serialize with cancellation, deletion and competing claims, using database time after lock waits. External delivery cannot be atomic with the database and must tolerate uncertain outcomes. |
| Infrastructure | Existing authenticated email verification and a safe pending inbox; no new invitation delivery worker. | Secure transactional outbox, worker, email provider, protected landing/claim experience, secret lifecycle, abuse controls and operational monitoring. |

## Proposed B protocol

1. At creation generate 32 cryptographically random bytes per invitation. Store only a SHA-256 digest in the invitation record, unique where appropriate. Preserve the existing 168-hour invitation lifetime as the proposed initial policy, measured using database time; require separate approval to change that policy. Do not return the secret to the sender or expose whether the recipient has an account. Deliver only to the address captured at creation, never to a client-supplied replacement address during retry.
2. Atomically create the capsule, invitation digest and a delivery job. A worker needs the raw secret to deliver it: a digest alone is insufficient. Store the payload encrypted in the transactional outbox using a server-only managed encryption key, unique nonce and authenticated encryption with job/invitation context as associated data. Limit access and retention, define key rotation and recovery, and delete ciphertext after terminal handling. Do not use an unprotected plaintext database job, logs or analytics as a token queue. The mail provider and mailbox necessarily see the secret. A separately reviewed alternative delivery design is needed if this key management is unavailable.
3. Use an HTTPS link with the secret in the URL fragment, so it is not sent in the initial HTTP request; the trusted landing page removes it from the address bar promptly. Do not put secrets in server paths, query strings, redirects or logs. Use `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, `Cache-Control: no-store`, no third-party scripts or tracking, a trusted fixed origin, strict content security and safe request/error logging. Browser history, extensions, screenshots, link scanners, mail-provider rewriting, phishing and compromised clients still present risks. Never consume on GET, page load or preview; a scanner must not claim. Only an explicit authenticated POST may consume it. Design sign-in handoff so a secret is not lost or exposed in a redirect; do not persist it in ordinary storage or logs.
4. FastAPI validates the session and derives the account identifier from its verified subject; it accepts no client-supplied claimant identity or email. Hash the submitted secret server-side and use a service-only RPC. The RPC locks the active profile and relevant capsule and invitation using a consistent documented lock order shared with deletion/cancellation. Capture database time **after** lock waits. Verify digest, pending/unclaimed state, expiration, revocation, cancellation/deletion and claimant eligibility; then consume the secret and bind the account in one transaction. The capsule lock must precede the invitation lock, matching existing direct cancellation/reveal. Validate the complete order against profile deletion and all existing RPCs; do not assume this proposal proves deadlock freedom.
5. A cancellation committed first denies a subsequent claim; a claim committed first binds the account, while eligible later cancellation still prevents reveal. Claim expiry governs pending claim, not an already valid binding. Require `now < expires_at`. Claimed history is terminal even if deletion clears the account reference; never reissue or rebind it automatically. Existing server-time reveal checks still apply and must be independently tested. Use generic unavailable responses, minimal metadata and no body before an authorized reveal.
6. Treat one successful consumption as final. A retry after a lost response must not consume again or enable another account; provide a separate authenticated lookup of **already-bound** metadata, not a reusable secret-based success response. Reissue is out of scope for the first slice: an expired, revoked or uncertain invitation requires a separately designed recovery flow. A worker checks cancellation/expiry before dispatch, but a concurrent or already-sent email cannot be recalled; the claim transaction remains authoritative. Delivery retries can duplicate email and must never duplicate or reactivate a claim.

OWASP's guidance for analogous email tokens recommends cryptographically random, single-use, time-limited tokens, rate limiting and referrer protection; these controls do not turn a bearer secret into proof of a particular person's identity. See [OWASP Forgot Password](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Forgot_Password_Cheat_Sheet.html) and [OWASP Email Validation and Verification](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Email_Validation_and_Verification_Cheat_Sheet.html).

## Minimum prerequisites and unresolved assumptions

- **Policy approval:** explicitly approve mailbox-delivered bearer possession as sufficient for intended-mailbox verification, despite forwarding, phishing, compromised accounts and mailbox reassignment. If the existing current confirmed-email requirement remains, this decision does not resolve it. Approve the proposed expiry, post-claim email-change behavior and no-reissue initial policy.
- **Identity and deletion:** prove the backend verifies the subject and that deletion/profile locking and stale sessions cannot leave a deleted account able to claim or reveal. The precise managed Auth deletion/revocation behavior remains unverified. An email change no longer determines the claim under B, but it can affect account recovery and must not silently transfer a bound capsule.
- **Delivery and abuse:** select and securely configure an email provider and sender identity, server-only key management, encrypted outbox, bounded worker leases/retries and safe logs. Apply create and claim rate limits, spam controls, adult consent and report/block and deletion/retention controls appropriate to beta. Provider delivery, mailbox confidentiality and operator handling are assumptions, not guarantees.
- **Security validation:** implement effective role/grant and direct DB/storage bypass tests, cross-account denial, secret non-disclosure and single-use, expiry boundaries, scanner GET behavior, cancellation/deletion and competing-claim races, no early body, worker failure/retry and lost-response scenarios. Verify the real deployed permissions and transactional behavior only in a separately authorized isolated environment. Keep the DB and API/mobile gates disabled until review and separate activation approval.
- **Client safety:** review the actual landing origin, fragment handling, browser/deep-link and sign-in transitions, CSP, caching, referrer behavior and logging at every proxy and provider. A token can still leak via an email, compromised client or user forwarding; no mechanism here prevents intentional sharing or provides end-to-end encryption.

## Next actual implementation task

After explicit approval of B's identity semantics and the schema/dependency scope, implement **only a dormant local claim core**: add a new draft migration after 0008 with a digest-backed invitation state and a service-only atomic claim RPC, plus a FastAPI claim contract that derives the verified subject and submits a digest. Keep the 0006 `CHECK (false)`, backend/mobile flags and public claim/discovery endpoints fail-closed; do not wire delivery, create real invitations or connect mobile. Use a separate disposable local test harness with synthetic fixtures and test-only gate relaxation, with safeguards against hosted/shared databases. Cover valid/invalid/replayed secrets, boundary expiry after lock waits, unauthenticated or deleted accounts, sender exclusion, terminal historical claims, claim/claim and claim/cancel/deletion races, projection and role denials, and existing self-capsule regressions. Under B, a different active account holding a valid secret is not distinguishable as the wrong person; tests must expose this limitation. Verify lock order with the existing functions. Implement outbox/delivery and the authenticated client handoff in later separately scoped slices before any end-to-end activation.

This task is concrete implementation work, but a local successful test would not prove provider/session deletion semantics, email delivery safety or production grants. No current gate should be weakened to make it pass.
