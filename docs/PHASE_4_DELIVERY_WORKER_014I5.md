# Phase 4 #014I-5: dormant invitation-delivery worker foundation

## Implementation overview

Added draft migration `services/api/migrations/0011_dormant_invitation_worker.sql` and independently usable verification primitives in `services/api/app/invitation_delivery_worker.py`. The migration keeps the validated 0006 `CHECK (false)`, adds lease fields to the draft outbox, and provides candidate and transition functions. It creates a dedicated `aftertime_invitation_worker` role with `NOLOGIN` and `NOINHERIT`, no membership or credentials, no direct table access, and execute privileges only on the worker functions. Public, anonymous, authenticated and existing service roles cannot execute them in the local test. This is a local draft and has not been applied to hosted Supabase.

The Python verifier authenticates AES-GCM with the stored salt and context, verifies the expected environment/version key ID and compares the SHA-256 digest using `hmac.compare_digest`. It returns a masked claim object only after validation and emits a fixed generic error for malformed, mismatched or tampered data. The envelope representation is redacted. `run_delivery()` always fails closed: there is no production worker loop, key provider, database connector or real mail transport. Tests use only a synthetic, in-memory fake mail transport. No email was sent, no production secret used, and no public direct route, claim or discovery path was enabled.

## Worker state machine and lock ordering

The outbox remains `pending` while queued or leased; a pending row has either no lease or a token and finite expiry. Terminal states are `sent`, `failed`, `cancelled` and `expired`. Terminal transitions clear ciphertext, lease and next-attempt time and record a database completion timestamp. Deleting a capsule/invitation cascades the outbox row; nonce/salt collision records remain. Clearing a column is not secure erasure of WAL, backups or memory.

The candidates function returns at most 100 due, unleased or expired-lease job IDs; it makes no eligibility promise. For a selected ID, the transition function locates the rows and acquires `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` locks in **capsule, invitation, outbox** order. Existing cancellation locks capsule then invitation; claim locks Auth/profile before capsule then invitation. The worker takes no Auth/profile locks and never reverses capsule/invitation ordering. It rechecks row identity, job state, lease token and authoritative `clock_timestamp()` after locks, including cancellation, deletion, revocation, claimed state, digest presence, invitation expiry and creation-time sanity. Locked rows are skipped and need a later poll. This reduces blocking but may delay or starve jobs under sustained contention; account-deletion cascades and all deployed lock paths still need review.

An ineligible job encountered during a valid lease/prepare/finish transition is marked cancelled or expired and its ciphertext cleared. Cancellation, claim and revocation functions do not immediately update the job; cleanup waits until the next eligible poll or a valid worker transition. No real dispatch may rely on that delay being safe.

## Lease and retry rules

The proposed trusted worker must supply a fresh, high-entropy UUID token for every lease; the draft SQL does **not** enforce token freshness across attempts, processes or restores. A lease lasts 60 seconds and increments `attempts` immediately, including if the worker crashes before preparing. A competing worker cannot acquire an active lease; an expired lease may be recovered with a fresh token. Old tokens and expired leases cannot prepare or finish a job. After five attempts, a further recovery or a retry becomes terminal `failed`. A retry before that clears the lease and schedules bounded exponential delays of 30, 60, 120 and 240 seconds; the formula caps at 300 seconds. `sent`, `fail`, cancellation and expiry clear ciphertext. Invalid keys/tampering/digest mismatch are fail-closed in the verifier; classification as permanent failure is a future worker decision.

`prepare` rechecks eligibility while holding locks and returns only the encrypted payload, delivery address, IDs and digest to the restricted worker role. The database transaction releases its locks before any external email can be sent. Therefore cancellation, revocation, claim, expiry or deletion can occur **after prepare and before or during send**. The current design cannot guarantee cancellation wins that race. No real delivery is wired until a separately reviewed authorization/locking policy addresses it. Even holding locks through a network call would introduce availability and uncertain-outcome tradeoffs and would not undo a delivered email.

A timeout after a provider accepted a send is ambiguous. Retrying may deliver the same invitation twice; the fake test demonstrates this. No exactly-once external delivery is claimed. Stable provider idempotency, duplicate-safe content and a reconciliation policy require a later design. A crash during an external send and an expired lease can also lead to a second sender; lease fencing prevents stale database updates, not a stale process from sending.

The current transition RPC returns a row only for a successful lease or eligible prepare; retry and terminal actions return no row whether they applied or were denied. A real worker cannot reliably acknowledge completion from this contract. Completion acknowledgements, token generation/non-reuse, worker identity provisioning, stale-worker fencing at the transport boundary, failure classification, reconciliation and transaction boundaries remain unresolved. The worker is deliberately unwired rather than treating these as established guarantees.

## Remaining blockers and next task

The dedicated database role has no login or membership; production role mapping, effective privileges and PostgREST/driver transaction behavior are untested. No root-key registry, restore/clone entropy guarantee, real mail provider, delivery worker, provider idempotency or operational logging controls exist. The current claim path does not verify the account's current email against the intended mailbox; this identity-policy requirement remains unresolved. The tests do not prove real managed Auth deletion/session races, exact-once delivery, external log secrecy, or a cancellation guarantee after `prepare`.

**Next task:** redesign the worker transition contract to return reliable committed acknowledgements, generate or enforce fresh fenced lease tokens, and explicitly decide cancellation-versus-send and ambiguous-outcome semantics. Then add local concurrent cancellation/claim/send-boundary tests with a fake provider and a rollback/commit-capable worker adapter. Keep `run_delivery`, the direct feature flag, 0006 gate and real email disabled until separate security review and authorization.
